0. Three technologies that travel together

In Pakistan, students already meet computing as LMS homework, ATM cards, hospital records, and weather apps for wheat. Three ideas sit under almost every “smart” headline.

Artificial Intelligence

Artificial = made by people. Intelligence = tasks that need judgement. Face unlock, YouTube recommendations, a website chatbot. AI is the “brain” of a smart system.

Internet of Things

Physical devices on a network that collect and share data and often act without a human in the loop: watches, meters, cameras, irrigation valves.

Data Analytics

Clean, study, and visualise the flood of readings so a human (or an AI) can decide. Bar and pie charts are the last mile of analytics, not the whole job.

\[ \text{IoT sensors} \xrightarrow{\text{data}} \text{Analytics} \xrightarrow{\text{patterns}} \text{AI decision} \xrightarrow{\text{command}} \text{Actuator} \]

Smart home: a temperature sensor reports $33^\circ\mathrm{C}$; analytics confirms it is a trend, not a glitch; AI switches the inverter AC on.

1. Anatomy of an IoT system

Sensors — they listen

  • Temperature, motion, smoke
  • Fingerprint / camera (biometric)
  • Soil moisture (farming)

A fingerprint sensor captures an image, builds a template, and compares it with stored data.

Actuators — they do

  • Motor, electric switch
  • Automatic door, irrigation valve

Motion detected $\to$ controller $\to$ motor opens the door. The actuator turns a bit into a movement.

1.1 Analytics pipeline

1
Collect from sensors, forms, logs.
2
Store in a file or database (Unit 4).
3
Clean duplicates, missing cells, wrong formats — dirty data lies.
4
Analyse totals, trends, outliers.
5
Visualise bar / pie / line so a non-programmer can see the story.

2. Information sources — not every page is a source

Table 2. Source ladder
TypeWhat it isExamplesTypical risk
PrimaryFirst-hand, originalNADRA / CNIC data, your interview, official registerIncomplete or false answers
SecondarySomeone already processed itTextbook, PBS report, newspaperOut of date, no citations
TertiarySummary of summariesEncyclopaedia, Wikipedia overviewOversimplified, unverified sites

A career page that lists “AI engineer, IoT technician, data analyst” is only as good as the organisation that published it. Ask: who wrote this, when, with what evidence, and what did they leave out?

4. Assistive technology — computing as inclusion

Assistive tools exist so disability does not become exclusion.

Mobility
Switch controls, adapted keyboards
Sensory
Screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver), magnification, captions
Communication
AAC devices and symbol boards
Cognitive / learning
Audiobooks, spell-check, text-to-speech

Related careers: assistive-software developer, AI/ML specialist, biomedical engineer, accessibility UX designer, rehabilitation technologist.

Self-check

  1. In one sentence each, separate IoT, AI, and analytics.
  2. Is a Wikipedia page a primary source? Why?
  3. Name one sensor and one actuator in a smart irrigation sketch.
Answers. (1) IoT gathers/acts in the physical world; analytics finds patterns; AI decides. (2) No — it is tertiary (sometimes secondary); it summarises other work. (3) Soil-moisture sensor; water-valve actuator.

Recap

  1. Smart loop: sensor $\to$ connect $\to$ process / analyse $\to$ AI $\to$ actuator $\to$ UI.
  2. IoT domains: city, industry, health, school, home, farm.
  3. Analytics is only as honest as its cleaning step.
  4. Primary / secondary / tertiary — always ask who, when, evidence.
  5. Every gain has a human cost; assistive tech is part of the answer, not an optional extra.
