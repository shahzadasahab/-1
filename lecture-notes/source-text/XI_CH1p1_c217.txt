is
a graphical tool used to simplify Boolean
expressions. It was invented by Maurice Karnaugh in 1953. K-maps help us reduce the

Why Use K-Maps?
When we have a Boolean expression with many terms, simplifying
it using
Boolean algebra can be difficult and time-consuming. K-maps provide a visual and
systematic method to find the simplest form.

The Explorer Panel:
The Explorer Panel on the left side of the window contains two sections. The
upper section shows your project structure, including all circuits in the project. The
lower section shows component libraries (Gates, Wiring, I/O, Memory, etc.) from
which you can select and place components onto the canvas. Clicking any component
in the Explorer Panel selects it for placement on the canvas.

(d)
The Attribute Table:
The Attribute Table appears below the Explorer Panel. When you click on a
component (either in the Explorer Panel or on the canvas), its properties appear in the
Attribute Table. For example, selecting an AND gate shows attributes such as Gate
Size, Number of Inputs, and Facing (direction). You can click on any attribute value to
change it.
(e)
The Drawing Canvas:
The Drawing Canvas is the large white area on the right side of the window.
This is where you design your circuit by placing components and connecting them with
wires. The canvas has a grey dot grid to help you align components neatly. You can
zoom in and out using Ctrl+= and Ctrl+- or using the mouse scroll wheel. The canvas
background is white by default; the grid dots are light grey and do not appear in printed
or exported versions of the circuit.
