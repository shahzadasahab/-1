The Information Age is a period where vast amounts of data are produced,
shared,
and accessed instantly through the
internet, mobile devices, and digital
platforms. In such an environment, simply having access to technology is not enough.
Students and users must develop the skills to understand, manage, and use information

Digital literacy goes beyond basic computer skills. It involves a combination of
technical, cognitive, and social abilities that enable individuals to function successfully
in a digital society.

The data gathered through a survey, investigation, or classroom project, can be

6.3.1
Qualitative Data:
Qualitative data describes what people think, feel, notice, or experience. It is
useful when the aim is to understand reasons, attitudes, and behaviour rather than
numbers or measures.
Examples include students' opinions about online classes, descriptions of study
habits, and observation notes written during a computer lab activity.

6.6
Designing a Data-Collection Approach:
Before collecting data, it is essential to design a data collection approach. A
data-collection approach
is the plan used to gather accurate, relevant, and useful
information for a study or project.
The data collection approach usually consists of the following steps.
Step 1: Define the research question clearly.
Step 2: Decide the type of data needed: quantitative, qualitative or both.
Step 3: Select suitable methods such as a survey, interview or observation.
Step 4: Choose the sample or target group.
Step 5: Prepare the tools such as forms, questions and observation sheets.
Step 6: Collect the data carefully and accurately.
Step 7: Store the data digitally in a spreadsheet and keep a backup copy.
Step 8: Follow ethical practice and respect privacy.

Step 10: Analyze the Results
After collecting responses from 40 students, a group may find that most students
use mobile phones for 3 to 5 hours daily. About half of the students may report that
they use mobile phones for educational purposes, such as watching lectures, searching
notes, and communicating with classmates. However, many students may also report
that social media notifications disturb their concentration while studying.
The survey may show that students who use mobile phones mainly for study
have better time management than students who use phones mostly for entertainment.
The results suggest that mobile phones can be useful learning tools if they are used
responsibly.
Step 11: Write the Conclusion
The research shows that mobile phones have both positive and negative effects
on study habits. They help students access learning materials, search for information,
and communicate with teachers and classmates. However, excessive use of social
media, gaming, and entertainment apps can reduce study time and concentration.
Therefore, mobile phones should not be avoided completely. Instead, students should
learn to use them wisely by setting time limits, turning off unnecessary notifications,
and using educational apps during study time.
