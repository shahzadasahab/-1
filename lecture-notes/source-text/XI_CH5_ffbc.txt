5.1.1 Artificial Intelligence:
Al is called “artificial” because it is created by humans, and
“intelligence”
means it can perform tasks that require thinking. AI systems use data to make decisions.
For example, when a mobile phone recognizes a face to unlock the screen, it uses AI.
When YouTube suggests videos according to a user’s interests, it also uses AI. When
a chatbot answers simple questions on a website, it is using AI.
AI in Smart Systems: Artificial Intelligence often works together with other
technologies like the Internet of Things (IoT) and Data Analytics. loT devices gather
data from the real world. Data Analytics sorts and studies this information. Then, AI
uses the results to make decisions.
For example, in a smart home, a temperature sensor collects data about the
room temperature. The system analyzes the data. If the room is hot, AI can decide to
turn on the fan or air conditioner. In this way, AI acts like the “brain” of a smart system.
5.1.2 Internet of Things:
The Internet of Things is a system in which devices are connected to the Internet
and can collect and share data to perform tasks automatically. IoT devices include
sensors, smart watches, smart cameras, smart meters, smart appliances, machines,
vehicles, and other connected devices. For example, a smartwatch collects data about
steps, heart rate, and sleep. It sends this data to a mobile phone. The mobile phone
shows the information to the user.
IoT relies on several foundational components that have been strengthened by
recent technological advancements.
i.
Sensors: Sensors are a vital component of IoT systems. Sensors are devices
that collect information from the physical world. They can detect temperature,
light, sound, movement, pressure, humidity, or other conditions. The following

5.1.3 Data Analytics:
Data Analytics is the process of collecting, organizing, cleaning, studying, and
presenting data to find useful information. Data Analytics helps people make better
decisions.
It is used in education, business, healthcare, government offices, banks,
agriculture, and many other fields.
For example, a college prepares examination results. Teachers collect marks,
correct mistakes, calculate percentages, and check which students need help.
It comprises five key components; each component plays a vital role in making an
accurate decision.
i.
Data Collection: This is the first step in the data analytics process. It collects
raw information from multiple sources, such as websites, apps, surveys, or
machines.
Examples of Data Sources:
School attendance records

may be stored in files, databases, spreadsheets, or in the cloud.
For example,
a college stores students’ admission records, attendance, and
examination results in a computer system or online storage facilities.

data. Raw data may contain errors, duplicate records, missing values, or wrong
formats. The following issues may arise during data collection and must be
identified and addressed.
e
Duplication of files or records.
e
Incorrect values, where

marks are missing or a roll number is typed incorrectly. These mistakes must
be corrected before final results are prepared.
